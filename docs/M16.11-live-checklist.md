# M16.11 — live verification checklist (by hand)

> **UPDATE 2 — a SECOND, worse bug was found by hand: the marker was one call behind.**
> Cause: the marker's position travelled in the URL *hash*, and a hash-only change is a
> same-document navigation — Chromium never reloaded, so the overlay's script never re-ran.
> Every new question drew the PREVIOUS question's marker; only repeating a question verbatim
> produced the right one. Fixed (query string + nonce), reproduced and re-verified by reading the
> live DOM.
>
> **This invalidated part of Update 1's results, and the re-assessment is in "Live results".**
> The `[main]` log lines were never wrong — they record what the app DECIDED, and the planner was
> correct every time. They were simply blind to what was DRAWN. Any check that ended in a marker
> was unverified; any check that ended in a refusal was unaffected.
>
> **UPDATE — a real bug was found and fixed by this checklist, and six checks now pass live.**
> See "Live results" at the bottom. The snapshot bug (the app naming ITSELF as the target window)
> is fixed; check 1 is its regression test. What still needs a human is **visual marker
> placement** — every result below was read from `[main]` log lines, which prove what the app
> DECIDED, not that a marker appeared over the right pixels.


The app is wired and boots. **These seven checks need a person at the keyboard**, for a reason
that is structural rather than incidental:

- **The marker cannot be photographed.** `PointerOverlay` sets `setContentProtection(true)`,
  which is `WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE` — it excludes the window from *every* capture API on the
  machine, not just this app's. M15 hit the same wall and verified by window enumeration; that
  only gives the overlay's window bounds (the whole display), not where the marker was drawn
  inside it. **Only an eye can confirm the marker landed on the control.**
- **The result sentence lives in the renderer.** It is not logged to stdout, and the command
  bar's own accessibility tree exposes nothing but `Chrome Legacy Window` (verified — see the
  note at the bottom, which is a finding in its own right).
- **Two checks are hand-timed** (alt-tab inside a ~1.9s window).

Setup: `POINTING_ENABLED=1` is already in `.env`. Run `npm run dev`. Hotkey is
**Ctrl+Shift+Space**. Have Notepad (several tabs, one named `Advice.txt`) and a File Explorer
window open.

---

## 1. Notepad — the File menu

> Focus Notepad. Press **Ctrl+Shift+Space**. Type `where is the File menu`. Enter.

**Expect:** marker frames the **File** menu, top-left of the Notepad window.
Sentence: `Pointing at "File" — top left of <window title>.`
Rect should be DIP **3,33 41x32** on the 1.5× display (native `4,49 62x48`).

- [ ] Marker is ON the File menu, not on Edit or the tab strip
- [ ] Time from Enter to marker felt roughly instant (~0.3–0.5s, no settle delay on native)

## 2. Notepad — the Advice.txt tab

> `point at the Advice.txt tab`

**Expect:** marker frames the whole **tab** (DIP **303,1 80x32**), *not* just the text label
inside it. This is the dedup rule: UIA exposes a 79×23 `Text` inside the 120×48 `TabItem`, and
the label must never be what gets pointed at.

- [ ] Marker covers the full tab, including its padding — not a smaller box around the words

## 3. Explorer — the New button

> Focus Explorer. `where is the New button`

**Expect:** marker on **New** in the toolbar. DIP **6,82 86x45** (native `9,123 129x67`).

- [ ] Marker is on New, not on "Add New Tab" (which also matches "New")

*(These three are the exact controls M15's vision grounding got wrong — one tab over, ~208px
onto a neighbour.)*

## 4. A Chromium/Electron app, deliberately

> Focus VS Code. `point at the source control icon`

**Expect:** a **noticeable pause** before the marker — one 350ms settle plus a ~1.5s read, so
roughly 1.5–2s total. That delay is the trigger-A tax: every Chromium window pays one settle on
window class alone, whether or not its tree was going to move.

- [ ] Marker lands on the Source Control icon in the activity bar
- [ ] The wait was noticeable but not annoying — **this is the one subjective judgement in the
      list, and it is the reason a human has to do it.** If it feels bad, the number to revisit
      is `SETTLE_MS` in `core/screen/settle.ts`.

## 5. The stale refusal — hand-timed

> Focus VS Code. Hotkey, type `where is the settings gear`, Enter — then **immediately alt-tab
> to another app** (you have ~1.9s).

**Expect:** no marker. A refusal reading:
`You've switched away from <window> — ask me again and I'll look at whatever's in front now.`

- [ ] No marker is drawn over the app you switched to
- [ ] The sentence names the window you asked about

*(If the marker draws anyway, that is the most important bug this checklist can find — it means
the always-on-top overlay is putting a confident marker over the wrong application.)*

## 6. `unreadable` — the bare tree

> Focus **Claude desktop**. `where is the send button`

**Expect:** `I can't read the controls in "Claude" right now — it isn't exposing them to
Windows' accessibility layer. You may need to find it yourself.`

- [ ] Wording is **present tense** — "right now", not "this app doesn't support it"
- [ ] It names the window

*(Claude desktop's tree was measured bare for 9.4s, fully populated an hour later, and bare
again the next day. If it happens to be populated when you try, it will just answer — that is
correct behaviour, and the reason the wording is present-tense. Force the case with any
Electron app that has just launched.)*

`unsettled` is the harder one to provoke by hand: fire the hotkey at a Chromium window in the
first second of it opening a heavy page. Expect `<window> is still loading — I can't read its
controls reliably yet. Ask me again in a second.`

## 7. Ambiguity — four Filter dropdowns

> Focus Explorer (Details view, so the column headers show). `point at the filter dropdown`

**Expect:** no marker. A refusal naming all four by **ordinal**, because all four share a name,
a control type *and* a position phrase:
`I can see 4 things that could be "the filter dropdown": "Filter dropdown" (1st from the left),
… (2nd from the left), … (3rd from the left) and … (4th from the left). Which one?`

- [ ] Four are named, by ordinal rather than by four identical position phrases

## 8. The app's own UI

> With the command bar open, ask `point at the send button` — or ask about anything while the
> bar is the only thing in front.

**Expect:** it reads whatever window was in front *before* the bar opened, never the bar. It
cannot target itself, by construction — the snapshot is taken before `showInput()`.

- [ ] It never points at the command bar
- [ ] With nothing else in front (bare desktop), it refuses rather than pointing at itself

## 9. The Notepad text area — a known gap, not a bug

> Focus Notepad. `point at the text area`

**Expect:** a refusal, most likely `I couldn't find "the text area" among the 26 controls I can
see in <window>.`

**This is intended.** Notepad's editing area is a `Document` measuring 1924×863 — 86% of the
window — and `MAX_WINDOW_FRACTION` drops anything at or above 50%, because a marker covering
most of the screen is not pointing at anything. Recorded so it does not surface later as a
surprise.

- [ ] It refuses rather than framing the whole window

## What was verified without a human (M16.11)

- The app **builds and boots** with the M16 wiring, and prints
  `[main] pointing ON - 'where is X' reads the focused window's control names and sends them to
  the model (no screenshot is taken)`.
- The **global hotkey is registered and fires**: a synthetic `Ctrl+Shift+Space` brought the
  command bar to the foreground, and typed characters plus Enter reached it.
- A **full pointAt run completed without crashing** — 16s of app life after the instruction, no
  errors, no unhandled rejections, process still healthy.

That is genuinely less than the checks above. Synthetic input proves the *pipeline* accepts a
real hotkey; it cannot prove a marker landed on a button.

## One finding from trying

The command bar's own window exposes **nothing but `Chrome Legacy Window`** to UI Automation —
no text, no controls. Two things follow:

1. It is a live confirmation of R1: an Electron window with a bare accessibility tree, this time
   our own.
2. It independently confirms the documented limitation that `pointAt` cannot usefully target
   this app's own UI — there is nothing in there to target, quite apart from the snapshot being
   taken before the bar opens.

It is also why the result sentence could not be read back automatically, which is why this
checklist exists.
